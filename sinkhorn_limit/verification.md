# Verification

- Scientific method checked using: the theory of matrix scaling. If $A$ has a positive diagonal, the Sinkhorn iteration converges (Sinkhorn and Knopp). The entries lying on no positive diagonal tend to 0, and the limit is the unique doubly stochastic scaling $D_1 A' D_2$ of the matrix $A'$ obtained by deleting those entries, which has total support.
  - An entry $(i, j)$ lies on a positive diagonal if and only if it lies on an alternating cycle with respect to one perfect matching of the support. The solution finds such a matching by augmenting paths, then tests strong connectivity in the associated directed graph (Tarjan). The mask agreed with brute-force enumeration of all permutations on 200 random sparse patterns.
  - The scaling solves $\mathrm{diag}(S\mathbf{1}) = I$ and $\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{1}^T S) = I$, with $S_{ij} = A_{ij} e^{u_i + v_j}$, by damped Newton steps in $(u, v)$, using the minimum-norm solution because of the gauge freedom $u + t$, $v - t$. It converges in 3 to 4 iterations, including on nearly decomposable matrices.
  - Plain Sinkhorn converges to the same limit, which confirms it, but slowly. On the 5×5 test matrix without total support, its error is exactly $2/k$ after $k$ sweeps ($2 \times 10^{-5}$ after $10^5$ sweeps). On the nearly decomposable 6×6 test matrix (couplings $10^{-6}$), it is still $7 \times 10^{-7}$ away after $2 \times 10^5$ sweeps.
  - Deliberate-error check: in a copy of the task, one realistic mistake at a time was introduced into `solution.py` and the tests were run. Each of these six mistakes made at least one test fail:
    - scaling by plain Sinkhorn with $10^4$ sweeps;
    - scaling by plain Sinkhorn until the row and column sums are within $10^{-13}$ (cap $10^5$ sweeps);
    - limit by plain Sinkhorn from $A$;
    - mask equal to all positive entries;
    - mask obtained by thresholding small entries;
    - strong-connectivity test using the column index instead of its matched row.
  - Applying Newton's method directly to $A$ without the mask is a valid alternative and passes every test: the log-scalings diverge exactly along the entries that must vanish.
- Frontier-model check: ChatGPT, given only `problem.md`, returned a solution that fails 1 of the 10 tests. Its mask, built from one matching test per entry, is correct, and so are its limits, because after masking the test matrices converge quickly. Its `doubly_stochastic_scaling` iterates Sinkhorn in log variables: on the nearly decomposable matrix it does not converge in $2 \times 10^5 + 10^4$ sweeps and raises `ArithmeticError`. With that error removed, its result is $5.6 \times 10^{-7}$ away from the reference, 5600 times the required accuracy.
- Expected answers checked using: an independent script that never calls `solution.py`.
  - Masks: brute-force enumeration of all $n!$ permutations ($9! = 362\,880$ for the largest case).
  - Doubly stochastic matrices: computed in 50-digit arithmetic (mpmath) by Gauss–Newton with an SVD pseudo-inverse, after a Sinkhorn warm start, to a residual below $10^{-45}$, on the matrix restricted to the brute-force mask.
  - Closed forms confirm several values: the $2 \times 2$ scaling, with diagonal $\sqrt{ad}/(\sqrt{ad} + \sqrt{bc})$; the 5×5 limit, whose two $2 \times 2$ blocks have diagonals $\sqrt{2} - 1$ and $\sqrt{6}/(\sqrt{6} + 1)$; an already doubly stochastic matrix, which is unchanged; and $\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, whose limit is the identity.
  - The solution matches the 50-digit references to within $2.2 \times 10^{-16}$ on every test.
- Numerical tolerances chosen because: the references are exact to double precision and the solution reaches about $10^{-16}$. `ATOL = 1e-10` (with `RTOL = 0`, since entries are in $[0, 1]$) is the accuracy stated in `problem.md`. Every failing method above misses it by at least $10^{-6}$. Masks are boolean and compared exactly.
- Important assumptions:
  - $A$ is nonnegative and has at least one positive diagonal, so the limit exists.
  - `doubly_stochastic_scaling` is only called on matrices with total support, where the scaled matrix is unique.
  - $n \le 12$, and positive entries lie between $10^{-8}$ and $10^{8}$.
  - The tests cover a positive matrix, an upper triangular pattern, a sparse 9×9 pattern with 12 entries off every positive diagonal, a $2\times 2$ closed form, an already doubly stochastic matrix, a nearly decomposable matrix, a matrix without total support, and a one-way coupling entry of size 1000 that must vanish.
