# Verification

- Scientific method checked using: since each $\varepsilon_i$ is symmetric, only $\lvert c_i\rvert$ matters, and the $k$ coefficients with the same magnitude $a$ contribute $a(2j - k)$ with weight $\binom{k}{j}$. The law is built by combining these groups with exact integer counts, merging equal values, and dividing by $2^n$. Even moments use $E[e^{xS}] = \prod_i \cosh(c_i x)$: with $\log\cosh\sqrt{y} = \sum_j \lambda_j y^j$ (exact rationals), $\sum_i \log\cosh(c_i x) = \sum_j \lambda_j \bigl(\sum_i c_i^{2j}\bigr) x^{2j}$; this series is exponentiated exactly with fractions and $E[S^{2m}] = (2m)!\,[x^{2m}]$. Everything is exact, so there is no rounding or cancellation. For even $p$ the ratio is formed as the exact fraction $E[S^p]/(\sum c_i^2)^{p/2}$ before a single conversion to float; otherwise it is $\bigl(\sum_s P(S{=}s)\,(\lvert s\rvert/\lVert c\rVert_2)^p\bigr)^{1/p}$, a sum of positive terms. Deliberate-error check: in a copy of the task, one realistic mistake at a time was introduced into `solution.py` and the tests were run. All eleven were caught: Gaussian moments $(2m-1)!!\,\sigma^{2m}$ (5 tests fail); power sums in `numpy` int64, which overflow silently (2); the moment in float64 (3); $m!$ instead of $(2m)!$ (6); $\ge$ instead of $>$ in the tail (2); zero coefficients dropped from the count of sign patterns (4); coincident values overwritten instead of added (7); $E[S^p]$ instead of $E\lvert S\rvert^p$ (3); the ratio through `float` of $E[S^p]$, which overflows for $p = 40$ (1); even $p$ recognised only when it is a Python `int`, and the law used for every $p$ (both do not finish within 60 s on $10^5$ coefficients).
- Expected answers checked using: an independent script that never calls `solution.py`. Laws: brute-force enumeration of all $2^n$ sign patterns ($n \le 20$) and, separately, the exact generating polynomial $\prod_i (z^{c_i} + z^{-c_i})/2$ (after division by the gcd), which agree. Moments: a recursion over groups of equal magnitude, $E[(X+Y)^{2l}] = \sum_i \binom{2l}{2i} E[X^{2i}]E[Y^{2l-2i}]$, with $E[(\varepsilon_1+\dots+\varepsilon_k)^{2l}] = 2^{-k}\sum_j \binom{k}{j}(2j-k)^{2l}$, all in exact integers; cross-checked against the generating polynomial on 20 coefficients and by hand for $c = (1, 2, 3)$: $E[S^4] = 3\cdot 14^2 - 2\cdot 98 = 392$. Ratios: 60-digit mpmath from these exact laws and moments; closed forms $72^{1/3}/\sqrt{14}$ for $c = (1,2,3)$, $p = 3$, and $1/\sqrt2$ for $c = (1,1)$, $p = 1$.
- Numerical tolerances chosen because: laws, moments and tail probabilities are exact rationals or integers and are compared with `==`. Ratios are compared with `RTOL = 1e-12`, the accuracy stated in `problem.md`; the references have 20 correct digits and a correct double-precision evaluation reaches about $10^{-15}$.
- Important assumptions: the coefficients are integers, so all values of $S$ are integers and equality between values is exact. The tests cover zeros, opposite coefficients and coincident values; 60 coefficients of order $10^{12}$ (121 atoms, $2^{60}$ sign patterns); $10^5$ coefficients with about 78 500 distinct magnitudes up to $10^6$ and $m = 20$ ($E[S^{40}] \approx 9 \times 10^{353}$, beyond float range); 1000 equal coefficients, where the Gaussian moment is off by 3%; $t$ equal to a value of $\lvert S\rvert$, below 0 and at the maximum; and $p = 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 7, 40$.
