# Verification

- Scientific method checked using: the Fourier decomposition $(1 - k^2)\,y_k = c_k$ of $y'' + y = \mu$. The non-resonant modes give $y_p$, the resonant forcing $a_1 \cos t + b_1 \sin t$ gives the secular term $\tfrac{t}{2}(a_1 \sin t - b_1 \cos t)$ with slope $-b_1/2$ at $t = 0$, and a free oscillation $A\cos t + B \sin t$ enforces $y(0) = y'(0) = 0$. The complete solution was compared with a direct numerical integration of the ODE (scipy `solve_ivp`, DOP853, `rtol = atol = 1e-13`). The maximum difference is $6 \times 10^{-14}$ (non-resonant), $4 \times 10^{-13}$ (pure resonance) and $6 \times 10^{-12}$ (all harmonics, up to $t = 60$). Deliberate-error check: in a copy of the task, one realistic mistake at a time was introduced into `solution.py` and the tests were run. Each of these eight mistakes made at least two tests fail: $1/N$ instead of $2/N$ normalization, sign of $b_1$ flipped, wave numbers from `np.fft.fftfreq(N, 1/N)` (not exactly 1 for $N = 49$), wrong sign of $y_p'$, no secular term, slope $-b_1/2$ forgotten, $y'(0) = 0$ not enforced, and free oscillation omitted.
- Expected answers checked using: an independent script that never calls `solution.py`. For trigonometric-polynomial forces: exact coefficients and hand-derived closed forms, $y_p = 0.25 - \tfrac{0.7}{3}\cos 2t + 0.05\sin 3t$ and $y = 0.75(\sin t - t\cos t)$ for $\mu = 1.5 \sin t$. For $\mu = e^{\cos(t-0.4)}$: the generating function $e^{\cos s} = I_0(1) + 2\sum_{k \ge 1} I_k(1)\cos ks$ (modified Bessel functions, `scipy.special.iv`), which gives $a_1$, $b_1$, $y_p$ and the full response as exact series. Every expected response was cross-checked against the direct ODE integration above.
- Numerical tolerances chosen because: the method is exact for the interpolant, so only rounding error remains (about $10^{-13}$), and the independent references agree to $6 \times 10^{-12}$. `RTOL = 1e-8` and `ATOL = 1e-9` leave three orders of magnitude of margin for any correct method (explicit DFT sums, variation of constants, exact quadrature), while every modelling error changes the result by at least $10^{-3}$.
- Important assumptions: $N$ is odd, so there is no ambiguous Nyquist term. $\mu$ is defined as the trigonometric interpolant of the samples, so the expected answers are exact for it; for $e^{\cos(t-0.4)}$ with $N = 49$ the interpolant equals the continuous force to machine precision. The tests cover no resonance, pure resonance, and a mixed force with all harmonics up to long times.
